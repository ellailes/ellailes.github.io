I'm interested in what large cosmological surveys can tell us about the
contents of the universe, and in the statistical methods needed to extract that
information cleanly.

My PhD work uses fast radio bursts as a probe of the intergalactic medium. Before
that I worked on two related threads: using stars and terrestrial detectors to
search for light hidden-sector particles, and building null tests and estimators
for cross-correlations between line-intensity-mapping surveys.

## Current work

My PhD research is on **fast radio burst cosmology**. FRB dispersion measures
encode the integrated free-electron column along the line of sight, which makes
a large, well-localised FRB sample a direct probe of the baryons in the
intergalactic medium &mdash; including the ones that galaxy surveys miss.
